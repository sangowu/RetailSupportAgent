# M5.2: how much thinking does this task set actually need

## 0. Why this step exists

The frozen configuration is `deepseek-v4-pro` with `reasoning_effort=high`, chosen in
M0.4 because it was the cheapest candidate that met the quality bar. Thinking was never
lowered from it. M0.4 tested the level **above** high and rejected it: `max` cost 13% more
and netted one extra task out of eighteen stable ones. The level below has never been run.

That matters now because M5.1 measured what thinking costs. On the `baseline-v3` run,
294 285 of the agent's 458 263 output tokens are reasoning, billed at the output rate,
and the agent's own model calls account for 59% of each task's wall clock with a p95 of
15.3 seconds. Thinking is the single largest lever on both cost and latency that does not
touch the business logic.

The counter-argument is equally measured, and it is the reason this step needs a design
rather than a run. M0.4 established that DeepSeek's thinking mode ignores `temperature`,
so the subject cannot be pinned. M5.1's instrument now shows what that means at full-set
scale: the two full runs of the **same** configuration on 2026-09-08 scored 105/114 and
102/114 and disagreed on 15 of the 114 tasks. A single run that comes back three tasks
lower is evidence of nothing.

## 1. Change card

- **Stage and step**: M5.2, the first M5 step that changes a configuration
- **Type**: optimization
- **The specific problem**: reasoning tokens are 64% of the agent's output and the agent's
  calls are 59% of each task's wall clock, and nobody has checked whether the task set
  needs that much thinking
- **The single theme of this change**: the thinking level of the agent under test, and
  nothing else
- **Control variables this change does not touch**: the model id, the prompt, the guard,
  the business implementation, the temperature that the server ignores anyway, the task
  sets, the simulated user (`glm-5.3-flash`, effort high), the judge (`qwen3.7-flash`,
  thinking off, json_schema), the upstream commit, the seed, the concurrency and the price
  snapshot. `bench_agent.py` is not touched: this step changes what the run is configured
  with, not what the subject does.
- **Explicitly out of scope**:
  - **No prompt change.** A shorter prompt would also cut tokens and would make the
    comparison unreadable. It is a separate step.
  - **No per-task routing.** Choosing a level per task is named in
    `docs/MODEL_EXPERIMENTS.md` as its own M5 feature, charged for its own cost and
    errors. Not here.
  - **No model change.** The candidate pool was settled in M0.4.
  - **No new baseline unless the change is accepted.** A rejected candidate is recorded
    and archived, never frozen.
- **Expected benefit**: fewer reasoning tokens, so a lower cost per successful task and a
  lower p95 on the agent's call
- **Possible cost**: quality. Retail tasks involve policy checks and multi-step
  cancellations, and less thinking may fail exactly the tasks the project claims to
  handle. Also the money the runs themselves spend.

### 1.1 The candidates

| Level | Sent as | Why it is in the experiment |
|---|---|---|
| `high` | `reasoning_effort=high` | the incumbent, already run twice on the full set |
| `low` | `reasoning_effort=low` | DeepSeek's lowest thinking level; never run |
| thinking off | `thinking.type=disabled` | the floor. If it holds, everything above it is waste |

`max` is not a candidate: M0.4 rejected it with a measured 13% cost for one task, and
this step does not reopen a settled question. `medium` and `xhigh` are not candidates
either: DeepSeek maps them onto `high`, so running them would measure nothing while
costing the same.

### 1.2 Design decisions fixed before the work

**The noise floor is measured before any candidate is judged, and it comes from runs that
already exist.** Two full runs of the incumbent configuration are on disk. Their paired
disagreement is the yardstick every candidate is held to. Any conclusion of the form "the
candidate lost N tasks" is meaningless until N is compared with what the incumbent loses
against itself.

**Screen small, then confirm large.** The first pass is 24 dev tasks stratified by judge
coverage, the same sample M0.4 used. A level that collapses there is rejected without
paying for a full run. A level that survives goes to all 114 tasks, which is the only
figure that can be compared with the baselines.

**Tuning happens on dev tasks only.** The 40 holdout tasks are not used to choose a level.
They are part of the full set, so the full run covers them; their subset score is reported
separately as the check on tasks that took no part in the decision.

**Runs go off peak.** M5.1 established that DeepSeek's peak rate is exactly twice its
off-peak rate. Peak is Beijing 09-12 and 14-18 on weekdays, which is 01:00-04:00 and
06:00-10:00 UTC. Running outside those windows halves the bill for the same experiment,
and every cost reported is priced at the moment each call was made regardless.

**The comparison is paired and reported on three axes**, with `scripts/compare_runs.py`:
quality by McNemar exact test, latency as p50 and p95 of the agent's own call, and cost
normalized to one tier so the hour of the run cannot flatter a candidate.

### 1.3 The decision rule, fixed before any result is seen

A candidate replaces `high` only if **all** of these hold:

| Condition | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Safety | unconfirmed writes stay 0, and no out-of-scope write appears |
| Quality, significance | McNemar exact p >= 0.05 against **both** incumbent full runs |
| Quality, size | the candidate's pass count is no more than 3 below the lower incumbent run, 3 being the measured spread of the incumbent against itself |
| Saving | normalized cost per successful task falls by at least 15%, **or** the agent call's p95 falls by at least 25% |
| Completeness | the run is complete: no unscored task, and no early stop |

If a candidate is cheaper but fails a quality condition, it is rejected and recorded. If a
candidate meets every condition, the level changes and a new baseline is frozen for it. If
the result lands inside the noise band with a large saving, one repeat run is made rather
than deciding on a single sample, and the decision is taken on the pair.

Anything that ends INCONCLUSIVE stays INCONCLUSIVE in the report. The incumbent is kept on
a tie, because a change that cannot be shown to be an improvement is a change without a
reason.

## 2. What was run

Four runs, all on 2026-09-09, all off peak, all on the frozen stack.

| Run | Configuration | Tasks | Passed | Spent, three roles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `20260909T100532Z` | `reasoning_effort=low` | 24 dev | 24/24 | 2.2579 CNY |
| `20260909T102214Z` | thinking disabled | 24 dev | 21/24 | 1.5397 CNY |
| `20260909T103513Z` | thinking disabled | 114 full | 97/114 | 7.1063 CNY |
| `20260909T113217Z` | `high`, the incumbent repeated | 114 full | 101/114 | 10.5866 CNY |

Total 21.49 CNY, against a planned 16 and a ceiling of 27. The fourth run was not in the
plan; 2.3 explains why it had to be.

### 2.1 `low` is not a saving, and it was rejected at the screen

| | `high` (same 24 tasks, from the full run) | `low` |
|---|---|---|
| Passed | 23/24 | 24/24 |
| Reasoning tokens | 55 401 | 62 179 |
| Normalized cost | 4.006 CNY | 4.261 CNY |
| Agent call p50 / p95 | 2.85s / 13.02s | 2.82s / 13.88s |

The parameter reached the server: `summary.json` records
`extra_body.thinking.reasoning_effort = low` as sent. What came back was **more**
reasoning, not less, at the same latency and the same quality, for 6% more money. There
is nothing to trade off, so `low` is rejected without a full run.

`docs/MODEL_EXPERIMENTS.md` warned about exactly this: accepting a parameter is not proof
the level applied. This is a measured instance of it.

### 2.2 The safety audit had to be built before any candidate could be judged

The decision rule requires zero unconfirmed writes, and M0.6 established that figure by
hand for one run, refusing "it cannot happen by construction" as evidence. Every later
step needs the same answer, so the hand method is now `scripts/audit_safety.py`: it
replays each task's recorded messages through the guard and, for every write that
actually reached the tools, asks whether the conversation before it confirmed that exact
fingerprint.

It reproduced both of M0.6's independently established numbers, which is what makes it
usable: 172 writes all confirmed for `baseline-v3`, and 168 writes none confirmed for
upstream's unguarded agent. Getting there took two corrections, and both are now tests:

- One held message can announce several blocked calls, and the guard keeps the **last**
  as pending. Keeping the first reported 13 confirmed writes in `baseline-v3` as
  unconfirmed.
- The pre-fix runs of 2026-09-08 asked for confirmation in Chinese. A parser that finds
  nothing must say it cannot audit the run, not that every write in it was unconfirmed.
  The script now refuses a verdict there and points at M0.6 section 5.6.

### 2.3 The change card's noise floor was wrong, and the run that would have hidden it

Section 1.2 of this card said the incumbent's spread could be read off two existing full
runs, 105/114 and 102/114. That was wrong, and the safety audit is what exposed it: the
105 run carries no confirmation prompt this script can read. Following that up in the
repository's own records, `docs/M0.6_AGENT_UNDER_TEST.md` section 5.6 says plainly what
it is - the run made **before** the confirmation fix, whose prompt was in Chinese and
whose negation matching misread seven confirmations. M0.6 archived it and stated it is
not a baseline.

So the gap between 105 and 102 is not run-to-run noise. It mixes noise with a code change,
and using it as a yardstick would have set the acceptance threshold from a measurement of
something else. The current configuration had exactly one full-set run, which is no
spread at all.

The repair costs one run: the incumbent, repeated, on the full set.

| | `baseline-v3` | the repeat | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passed | 102/114 | 101/114 | 1 task |
| Tasks that disagree | | | 11 (5 fixed, 6 broken) |
| McNemar exact p | | | 1.0000 |
| Normalized cost | 20.305 CNY | 20.068 CNY | -1.2% |
| Agent call p50 / p95 | 2.86s / 15.30s | 2.83s / 15.32s | unchanged |

The incumbent's own spread at full-set scale is **1 task in the score and 11 tasks in
which ones**. That is the yardstick the decision rule needed, and it is now measured
rather than assumed. The 3-task allowance written into the card before the work turns out
to be generous rather than strict.

### 3.2 Neither p-value says the two are the same

Both tests come back not significant, and on the significance condition alone the
candidate would pass. That is the trap this step was designed against: 19 and 20
disagreeing tasks are not evidence of equivalence, they are too few to resolve a
five-task difference. The measured spread of the incumbent against itself, one task, is
what gives the difference a scale.

Classifying every task by whether the incumbent is stable on it, the way M0.4 did:

| Category | Tasks | Thinking disabled |
|---|---|---|
| Incumbent passes in both runs | 96 | loses 10: 2, 3, 4, 12, 21, 27, 28, 36, 82, 111 |
| Incumbent fails in both runs | 7 | wins 4: 49, 64, 79, 98 |
| Incumbent disagrees with itself | 11 | passes 7 |

**Net on the 103 tasks where the incumbent is stable: minus 6.** The 97 is flattered by
winning 7 of the 11 tasks that are noise in any case.

### 3.3 What it actually gets wrong

Of the 10 tasks it loses that the incumbent always passes, **6 fail on the database
check**: the wrong end state, not the wrong wording. The other 4 have the correct
database state and fail an NL assertion.

The screen showed the failure mode plainly. Tasks 2, 3 and 4 all fail the same assertion:
the user asks how many t-shirt options are available, the correct answer is 10, and
without thinking the agent answers 11, 11 and 12 in the three tasks. The tool results are
right there in the conversation; what is missing is the counting. That is not verbosity or
style, and a customer told there are 12 options when there are 10 has been given a wrong
answer.

### 3.4 The decision rule, applied

| Condition | Threshold | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety | 0 unconfirmed writes | 178 writes, 0 unconfirmed, by replay | pass |
| Quality, significance | p >= 0.05 against both | 0.3593 and 0.5034 | pass |
| Quality, size | at most 3 below the lower incumbent | 4 below (97 against 101), and -6 on stable tasks | **fail** |
| Saving | -15% cost or -25% p95 | -33.7% per successful task, -72% p95 | pass |
| Completeness | every task scored | 114/114, no early stop | pass |

Four of five conditions hold. The rule requires all five.

## 4. Conclusion

**FAIL for the change: thinking stays at `high`.** Both candidates are rejected, for
opposite reasons.

- `low` buys nothing. It costs 6% more, returns more reasoning tokens than `high` despite
  the lower setting, and changes neither latency nor quality.
- Thinking disabled buys a great deal - a third off the cost per successful task and a
  p95 of 4.24s against 15.30s - and pays for it by getting six business outcomes wrong
  that the incumbent gets right every time. The project's claim is business correctness.
  That is not a trade this step is allowed to make.

What this step establishes, beyond the rejection:

1. **The incumbent's spread is one task in 114**, measured, not assumed. Every later
   comparison has a yardstick now.
2. **A safety audit that reproduces M0.6's hand count** and refuses to judge a run it
   cannot read.
3. **A recorded price for thinking on this workload**: it is 64% of the agent's output
   tokens, 37% of the cost, and 11 seconds of p95, and it buys six correct database
   outcomes out of 114 tasks.

Point 3 is a real option rather than a dead end, and it is deliberately left as one: a
fast, cheap mode with a stated quality cost is a product decision, not a default. If it is
ever wanted, this report is the measurement it would rest on, and the tasks it breaks are
listed by number.

### 4.1 Not verified

- **One run per candidate.** The incumbent now has two full runs; thinking disabled has
  one. Its own spread is unmeasured, so the -6 on stable tasks carries the incumbent's
  uncertainty only.
- **Other levels.** `max` was rejected in M0.4 and not re-run. `medium` and `xhigh` are
  mapped to `high` by DeepSeek and were not run. Nothing here says anything about
  thinking budgets expressed in tokens rather than levels.
- **Other models.** The candidate pool was settled in M0.4 and this step changed one
  parameter of one model.
- **Whether `low` is honoured at all.** The request carried it and the response did not
  visibly change. Whether the server ignored it, mapped it, or applied something that
  happens to cost the same cannot be told from the outside, and this step did not ask the
  provider.
- **The judge.** 12 of the candidate's failures involve NL assertions, and judge noise was
  measured at 8% in M0.4. That is smaller than the effect here but it is not zero, and no
  judge re-check was run on these results.
